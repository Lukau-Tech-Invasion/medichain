# Absa Youth Entrepreneurship Fund: pre-application research for MediChain

Research date: **11 October 2026, South Africa**. This is a preparation brief, not an application or acceptance of consent. No applicant data was entered, no declarations were checked, no application was submitted, and no organiser was contacted.

## Decision

This is the **Absa Youth Entrepreneurship Fund (AYEF)**, delivered by **Tshiamo Foundation NPC**, separate from FNB App of the Year. The strongest provisional route for MediChain is **Stage 1: Idea to Proof of Concept**, if the applicant business is not currently trading and generating revenue. A developed application can still fit this stage. Confirm the applicant business's actual trading/revenue position before choosing; the last discussion about external user verification does not establish its financial status.

The opportunity is to turn an implemented healthcare application into a sustainable enterprise. The application should connect a specific customer, a practical product benefit, one achievable funding milestone, a realistic itemised budget, and the founder's capacity to execute and learn. It should not read like an awards feature catalogue.

## Eligibility: verify every item before proceeding

Pack p4 and the live form identify the following:

- Black South African citizenship.
- Age **18-35 at application**, not older than 35 when applying.
- Business based in South Africa.
- Applicant is its founder or a key decision-maker.
- Applicant is not employed by Absa, Tshiamo Foundation **or Tshiamo Impact Partners**. The form landing omits Impact Partners, but the pack explicitly includes it.
- One application per applicant. Pack screening examples also flag an ID or business that has already applied. Do not submit multiple ventures or cofounder duplicates without written clarification.
- Willingness to participate in assessments, learning, coaching, reporting and funding-use support.

Do not assume personal eligibility from the codebase, project name, location or screenshots. Citizenship, age, ownership and excluded employment have not been confirmed in this research. The website also describes the target enterprises as Black-owned; the precise shareholding threshold and treatment of mixed ownership were not found and should be clarified if relevant.

Pack p4 gives common screening failures: age/location mismatch, applying for someone else's business, excluded employment, duplication, required sections left incomplete and unwillingness to participate. Its examples are not exhaustive; the committee retains selection discretion.

| Stage | Published fit | Funding cap shown on live form | Places in pack p5 | Delivery |

| 3: Early Growth | Consistent trading, demonstrated traction and credible growth opportunity | **Up to R240,000** | 10 | Intensive support including in-person participation |

These are ceilings, not automatic awards or an instruction to request the maximum. The grant amount should follow the costed milestone. The three pathways run concurrently; applicants select a stage and the panel confirms placement. Do not add the three caps together or assume progression through all stages produces three awards.

Pack p6 explicitly allows zero revenue at idea stage. Code maturity, registrations, planned AWS hosting and a large number of pages are not substitutes for trading evidence when selecting Stage 2/3.

## Funding terms and support

The official FAQ describes grant funding without equity or ordinary loan repayment. Funding is conditional and phased against agreed plans. The live declaration and pack p11 establish that there is **no application fee**, no guaranteed selection and ongoing participation obligations.

Support covers business learning, coaching/mentorship, financial and governance capabilities, market readiness and connections. Stage 1 concentrates on the business model and understanding customers; Stage 2 focuses on operations and commercial development; Stage 3 on scaling and investment readiness (pack p5).

Unknown until detailed terms/contracting: precise disbursement schedule, eligible expense categories, treatment of founder remuneration, hardware/cloud/security spend, procurement rules, retrospective costs, proof of expenditure, taxes, unused funding, breach/clawback conditions, banking requirements before payment and frequency/attendance obligations. A grant described as non-repayable does not settle every contractual scenario.

## How selection works

Pack p3 describes initial application, eligibility/stage screening, assessment, verification and placement. Pack p9 gives two assessment lenses:

1. **Founder:** initiative, commitment, response to feedback and action already taken.
2. **Enterprise:** stage fit, evidence and ability to use the funding/support productively.

Both matter. The pack explains that business assessment confirms stage, while founder assessment distinguishes applicants within that stage.

### Published assessment emphasis (pack p9)

| Dimension | Where emphasis increases | Application implication |
|---|---|---|
| Clear problem and customer | Especially idea stage | Choose a particular buyer and workflow, rather than everyone/every hospital |
| Evidence of validation and trading | Especially trading stages | State evidence accurately and distinguish tests from sales |
| Founder capability and commitment | Especially idea stage | Show completed work and openness to coaching |
| Readiness to execute | With business maturity | Match milestone to current capability |
| Funding need and spend logic | With maturity | A traceable, proportionate budget |
| Growth, employment and market access | With maturity | A plausible pathway, not invented jobs or revenue |

No numeric score weights, minimum pass score or selection probability are published. Cohort balancing includes provinces, rural/peri-urban/township coverage, sectors and an aim for at least half women. The panel also considers where the capital has genuine additional value compared with applicants already well served by funding. State geography and other support honestly; do not manipulate a demographic or financial claim to fit a preference.

## Recommended MediChain narrative — brainstorming only

**Working product description:** MediChain is a healthcare application that connects patient access to records and consent with role-specific work for clinicians and supporting care teams. Its commercial proposition is continuity of information across care tasks, with patient-visible accountability.

**Buyer hypothesis to investigate:** A South African clinic or care organisation that coordinates records, reviewed diagnostic results and staff handovers. Patients are users/beneficiaries; the facility may be the paying customer. This is a hypothesis, not a current customer claim.

**Proposed milestone:** Qualify a hosted, demonstration-ready version of the core patient-to-care-team journey and complete a structured assessment of its usability and buyer fit. Refine the scope after the actual form and allowed expense terms are confirmed. Do not combine unrelated products or promise all specialist pathways as complete.

**Proposed evidence of execution:** Existing repository, dated implementation and selected synthetic workflow records, then a current hosted-build demonstration when available. Only report independently observed or measured results once they exist. Current material does not establish external adoption or clinical effectiveness.

**Support needs to consider:** Healthcare customer discovery and market access; commercial packaging and pricing research; financial/compliance readiness. Select the top three that genuinely address the founder's largest gaps.

**Budget framework (recommended, not an official approved list):** deployment/hosting and recoverable backups; scoped workflow/security verification; accessibility/usability work; customer-discovery activities; necessary specialist advice. For each proposed item record quantity, unit-cost source/date, total, deliverable and relation to the one milestone. Check whether each category is fundable before finalising. Avoid unsupported prices, double funding and treating temporary cloud credits as permanent free infrastructure.

**Economic contribution:** Explain how a sustainable facility offering could eventually support implementation, customer-support and product work. Describe potential roles as future possibilities linked to actual contracts and affordability. Do not claim current employment or guaranteed hiring that has not been established.

**Business risk:** Healthcare adoption requires trust, usable workflows, appropriate data handling and buyer validation. Describe how the funded milestone reduces those uncertainties. The research does not certify clinical safety, regulatory compliance or production readiness.
