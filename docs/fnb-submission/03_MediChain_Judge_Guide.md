# 03 MediChain Judge Guide

Prepared 7 October 2026. Research and positioning workshop. Not a final submission.

## Explore the connected care journey
A short guide for hands-on judging | Access edition awaiting hosted details

### Start with the patient
See what information the patient can view and how access is explained. Then switch to a professional role to see how that context supports a task.

### Structure status
This is the proposed structure for a later judge guide. After AWS hosting, add the verified URL, privately issued synthetic accounts, support contact and exact build through the final submission workflow.

### Use separate browser profiles
Patient, clinician, nurse, pharmacy, lab and responder accounts should use separate profiles or devices to avoid overwriting a shared session. Start each account at its own assigned portal.

### Demo data only
Use the assigned synthetic patient. Older local reports use Naledi Mokoena (Demo), PAT-DEMO-090; the hosted fixture may differ. Do not enter real patient data or call real emergency services.

### What to look for
The same patient context should follow the record, diagnostic evidence, treatment, handover and disclosure history. Reload a view to confirm the information persists.

## The ten-minute exploration
A suggested route, not an organiser-mandated presentation length.

### 0:00-2:00 | Patient perspective
Sign in to the patient portal. Open Records and Access History. Inspect a document and its recorded access information. Demonstrate a consent change only with the assigned fixture and restoration instructions.

### 2:00-4:00 | Clinician and diagnostic evidence
In the staff profile, find the same patient and open the chart. Inspect the reviewed lab document, observations and one stored clinical note. Ask whether the source information survives refresh.

### 4:00-5:30 | Treatment continuation
In the pharmacist profile, inspect prescription processing and dispensing history. Distinguish prescribed, dispensed and reconciled medicines.

### 5:30-7:30 | The incoming shift
Inspect a synthetic SBAR handover from the incoming nurse account. Follow the patient association and retained information. Unqualified flowsheet/IV/Morse pathways should not be presented as certified.

### 7:30-9:00 | Emergency context
Use the assigned simulated tag and authorised responder device. Inspect essential context and the handover patient link. Review the patient-visible emergency disclosure afterwards.

### 9:00-10:00 | Wider capability
Inspect role navigation and the capability appendix. Ask about adoption and operating cost hypotheses, integration readiness and the next validation milestone.

## What the demonstration should prove
Evaluate the product through observable transitions.

### Product and usability
Can an unfamiliar user find the record, finish a task and understand the next step? Does information read back after refresh and under the correct role?

### Creativity and impact
Does combining patient agency with care-team continuity offer a useful approach? The intended impact is fewer information gaps; measured health outcomes are not claimed.

### Sustainability
Is there a credible buyer segment and support/cost model? The facility-subscription model is a positioning hypothesis to investigate.

### Presentation and intelligence
Does the story demonstrate the app itself? Are alerts and scores explainable from inputs? No autonomous diagnostic or trained predictive-model performance is claimed.

### If a step fails
Record the role, build, action and visible error. Distinguish a live product failure from a recorded walkthrough. A video may explain intended behaviour; it cannot replace required usable judge access.

### Reference
Official judging domains are published in the organiser FAQ [S1]. No numeric weight or pass threshold was found. The ten-minute route is an entrant recommendation.
