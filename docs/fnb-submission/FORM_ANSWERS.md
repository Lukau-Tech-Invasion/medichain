## Short description (about 150 words)

> MediChain is a South African health record and emergency-access platform. When a patient arrives
> unconscious, a paramedic or emergency doctor can scan the patient's QR code with any camera,
> tap their NFC card on an Android phone, or type the card number. Blood type, allergies,
> medicines and emergency contacts then appear on an approved device, under a time-limited grant.
> Patients see every access to their record: who opened it, when and why, emergency access
> included. They can also grant or withdraw a clinician's access, sign consent and message their
> care team. Doctors, nurses, pharmacists, lab technicians and paramedics work on one shared
> record: notes from templates, orders, prescriptions, results and handovers. Every access is
> sealed into a tamper-evident audit trail. MediChain is designed for POPIA and hosted in Cape
> Town. All data in the demonstration is fictional.

## One-line summary

> Your health record goes with you, and you can see every access to it.

## What problem does it solve?

> Missing and paper-bound records delay treatment in South Africa. A study of 40 Limpopo
> hospitals found that missing records meant patients waited longer and were sometimes treated
> without their medical history (Marutha & Ngoepe, SAJIM 2017). In emergencies, the information that
> matters most (allergies, blood type, medicines) is often unavailable. Patients rarely know who has
> read their record. MediChain puts the emergency summary in a responder's hands in seconds, and
> shows the patient every access.

## What makes it innovative?

> - Emergency access needs an approved device, a recorded reason and a time limit. It is not just a
>   role, and the patient sees it afterwards.
> - The patient's access history is built in, not added on. Every access is sealed into
>   signed audit batches that are exported to write-once storage.
> - It works in three ways at the bedside: a QR code read by any camera, an NFC card on Android, or
>   the card number typed in.
> - Without a connection, the QR code still shows a printed summary, clearly labelled as unverified.
